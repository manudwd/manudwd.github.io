The Journey

My path began at the intersection of hardware and intelligence. Starting with silicon-level design at IBM, I learned that real innovation happens when you understand every layer, from the transistors to the algorithms.

At Michigan State, I found my focus: making AI efficient enough to run on the edge. I cut Generative-AI compute by 45% on FPGAs without pruning or quantization, proving that smarter design beats brute force.

Today, as Director of Engineering for AI/ML R&D at Tiami Networks, I lead a different paradigm: using the wireless signals already around us (5G-NR, LTE, DVB-T broadcast TV, Wi-Fi) as a sensing medium. Instead of deploying dedicated radar, we turn existing infrastructure into passive radar, an Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC) system that detects drones, tracks light aircraft to 25 km, and senses people, with no new RF hardware.

I own these products from research to the field: the DSP (cross-ambiguity, CFAR, clutter cancellation), the GPU/FPGA real-time processing, the machine learning, and the standards-grade C2 that gets detections to operators. Two IEEE publications, two patents pending, MWC showcases, and real customer deployments across telecom and defense. Every layer serves one purpose: technology that protects people.

CoreDiff

Most detectors learn what attacks look like; CoreDiff learns what normal looks like and measures how far a thing sits from it. Network activity is modeled as directed interaction edges, actor to endpoint:protocol:port, each a short time series of windowed KPI metrics. A self-supervised encoder compresses an edge's recent history into a latent vector, and a diffusion model is trained to denoise only benign latents, which makes it a learned probability density over the manifold of normal behavior. An edge is anomalous when it lands in a low-likelihood region of that density, so the detector flags zero-day attack families it was never trained on, with no signature.

A light supervised signal then sharpens the score without erasing that generalization: a margin term pushes labeled attacks away from the normal-latent center while a hard-negative term pins deceptive-but-benign traffic in place, trained jointly with the density objective. The density, prediction, reconstruction, graph and margin signals are z-standardized and fused by logistic calibration into one probability, and the alert threshold is fit from the empirical false-alarm-rate curve, giving a calibrated score with an explicit false-positives-per-hour operating point before each detection is attributed to a root cause with MITRE ATT&CK / STIX. Developed under the PIN-Cyber program.

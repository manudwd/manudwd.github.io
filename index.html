About

Operators already own the most widely deployed radio infrastructure in the world. My work is about getting more out of it. At Tiami Networks I lead the AI/ML research and engineering that turns that infrastructure into products: radar sensing on live 5G base stations, passive radar that can be set up anywhere for counter-UAS, RF digital twins that plan sensing before anything is deployed, anomaly detection that protects 5G SA and private networks, and world models that sell autonomy to robot and drone fleets as a network service.

All of it runs on one shared stack: a radar foundation model, an RF digital twin, and an edge deployment path that takes PyTorch research to licensed containers inside operator networks. I own the roadmap, the hiring, and the delivery, from first experiment to field trial with operators such as Vodafone and AirSpan.

Before Tiami, I worked on hardware for AI: generative-AI acceleration on low-power FPGAs at Michigan State University (45% less compute without pruning or quantization) and open-ISA processor accelerators with the OpenPower Foundation at IBM.

PolyRAN: Radar Sensing on an Operator's Existing 5G Network

Turns live 5G base stations into a wide-area radar without new radios, antennas, or changes to the RAN vendor's software. A sensing application (an O-RAN dApp) reads uplink reference signals the base station already schedules through a vendor-agnostic interface, and the radar foundation model turns them into detections and tracks. Integrated with OpenAirInterface, OCUDU, and NVIDIA Aerial.

Trialled publicly with Vodafone at its Málaga R&D lab on live 5G, detecting objects and people without disrupting calls, messages, or data. Also trialled with AirSpan and tier-2 operators in Asia and the EU.

Application: the operator dashboard fusing radar detections with Remote-ID, ADS-B, and satellite tracks on one map (demo data shown).

PolyEdge: Rapidly Deployable Passive Radar for Counter-UAS

A standalone sensor for sites where the operator network cannot be modified. It listens to existing 5G downlink signals, so it needs no transmitter and no spectrum license, and runs the same radar foundation model for detection, clutter rejection, and classification. Shipped as licensed containers (Docker, Helm, OpenShift) to major US and EU operator sites.

Detects small drones at about 1 km (below 400 ft, up to 15 m/s, radar cross-section about 0.01 m²). Tracks reach command systems over SAPIENT, ASTERIX, FAAD C2, and TAK.

Agentic RF Planner: Digital Twins for ISAC

Builds a city-scale RF digital twin from map and 3D building geometry. Vision-language agents read street-level imagery to assign building materials and clutter, and a physics-based propagation model (3GPP TR 38.901) plans communication coverage and radar sensing coverage on the same scene, showing where sensing will work before deployment.

Field-validated against commercial phone measurements in North America, Europe, and Asia across eight bands. Representative case: 4.22 dB mean absolute error over 17,324 measurements.

CoreDiff: Zero-Day Anomaly Detection for 5G SA and Private Networks

Converts any network into a structured graph of which components interact, and how, over time. A self-supervised model learns only normal behavior, and a latent diffusion model scores how unusual each interaction is, so new attack types are caught without signatures. Alarms are calibrated to a set false-alarm rate per hour, and each alert maps to MITRE ATT&CK. Developed under the PIN-Cyber program.

At the 1% anomalous tail: 5G SA about 94% F1, with time to detect cut from 20 to 5 minutes. Private networks about 98.9% F1, with mean time to detect cut from 120 to 0.6 seconds, across 13 ATT&CK techniques.

CoreDiffWFM: World Models That Turn ISAC into Autonomy Services

Gives operators a service they can sell on top of their network: robots and drones that lack autonomy (no map, no global position, poor sensor coverage) receive it from the network. The world model fuses ISAC and PolyEdge detections, network positioning, fixed cameras, and each agent's own sensors into one shared picture, and returns goals, holds, and keep-out zones that the agent's own navigation executes. Agents' observations fill the picture where infrastructure coverage is poor.

Indoor: a ground robot with no map or position is localized from fixed cameras and its lidar, and no goal is sent until position and keep-out zones exist. Outdoor: drone and ground-robot fleets over city geometry, where a hazard seen by one agent replans every affected mission.

The RF planner and a ray-traced physics model predict radio conditions along each route at every time step, explaining how the network will behave for an agent that must keep its link through a mission. A predicted 1.000 m move was observed as 0.980 m on a robot testbed; in simulation, three drones avoided a helicopter and cut fleet completion from 152 to 125 seconds.

ARCHES: End-to-End Counter-UAS Response

Closes the loop from detection to response. A radar cue hands off to an interceptor drone that tracks the target with onboard vision (YOLOv8) and Remote-ID, working with commercial drones without a vendor SDK. A staged safety process takes flight control from simulation to real flight on NVIDIA Jetson, with a ground station coordinating several drones.

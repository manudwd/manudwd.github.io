PolyRAN: Radar Sensing on an Operator's Existing 5G Network

Turns signals that live 5G phones already transmit into radar, running as an application on existing base station compute with no changes to the vendor's L1/L2 stack and zero added hardware. A sensing application (an O-RAN dApp) reads uplink reference signals through a vendor-agnostic interface, and the shared radar model turns them into detections and tracks. Integrated with OpenAirInterface, OCUDU, and NVIDIA Aerial.

Commercial trial with Vodafone on a live 5G network for pedestrian safety, detecting objects and people without disrupting calls, messages, or data. Licensing agreements with Tier-2 mobile operators in the US and Asia.

PolyEdge: Rapidly Deployable Passive Radar for Counter-UAS

A standalone sensor for sites where the operator network cannot be modified. It listens to existing 5G and LTE downlink signals, so it needs no transmitter and no spectrum license, and runs the same shared radar model for detection, clutter rejection, and classification. Shipped as licensed containers (Docker, Helm, OpenShift) to customer sites.

The first prototype (2024) reached 200 m against commercial base stations from 600 MHz to 3.8 GHz. A learned clutter prior, with per-site parameters from the RF planner's digital twin, extended range to 600 m to 1 km (below 400 ft, up to 15 m/s, radar cross-section 0.01 m²). Tracks reach command systems over SAPIENT, ASTERIX, FAAD C2, and TAK.

Agentic RF Planner: Deployability through Digital Twins

Builds a city-scale RF digital twin from map and 3D building geometry. Vision-language agents read street-level imagery to assign building materials and clutter, and a physics-based propagation model (3GPP TR 38.901) plans communication coverage and radar sensing coverage on the same scene, showing where sensing will work before deployment.

Field-validated against commercial UE and signal analyzer measurements in North America, Europe, and Asia across eight bands: 4.22 dB RSRP mean absolute error on n41 SA. Extended to broadcast TV (ATSC 1.0, ATSC 3.0, DVB-T) to site AirRadar deployments.

AI-on-RAN World Model: Network-Delivered Autonomy

Lets the network supply autonomy to robots and drones that have none of their own (no map, no global position, poor sensor coverage). The world model fuses ISAC and PolyEdge detections, network positioning, fixed cameras, and each agent's own sensors into one shared picture, and returns goals, holds, and keep-out zones that the agent's own navigation executes. Agents' observations fill the picture where infrastructure coverage is poor.

Indoor: a ground robot with no map or position is localized from fixed cameras and its lidar, and no goal is sent until position and keep-out zones exist. Outdoor: drone and ground-robot fleets over city geometry, where a hazard seen by one agent replans every affected mission.

The RF planner and a ray-traced physics model predict radio conditions along each route at every time step, explaining how the network will behave for an agent that must keep its link through a mission. A predicted 1.000 m move was observed as 0.980 m on a robot testbed. Three drones rerouted around a non-cooperative helicopter, completing the fleet mission 18% faster; ground robots completed 100% of missions around a blockage versus 50% for the baseline.

CoreDiff: Signature-Free Zero-Day Threat Detection

Converts any network into a structured graph of which components interact, and how, over time. A self-supervised model learns only normal behavior, and a latent diffusion model scores how unusual each interaction is, so new attack types are caught without signatures. Alarms are calibrated to a set false-alarm rate per hour, and each alert maps to MITRE ATT&CK. Developed under the Army C5ISR PIN-Cyber program.

PIN-Cyber Phase 1, at the 1% anomalous tail: 94% F1 on 5G SA, with time to detect cut from 20 to 5 minutes, and 98.9% F1 on private networks, with time to detect cut from 120 to 0.6 seconds, across 13 ATT&CK techniques. Phase 2 evaluates the detector inside the PIN-Cyber system on air-gapped networks.
